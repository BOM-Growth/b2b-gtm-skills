# Measurement and reporting

Status: draft
Playbook 15 of 17. Stage: Operate. Usually runs after the CRM can produce the numbers, before the second quarter of execution. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

Three 90-day objectives with measurable key results, a weekly scorecard where every number has a source and an owner, a 30-minute weekly go-to-market meeting that reads it, a monthly review and report, and a 90-day reset.

## Why it matters

Revenue is the result of what was done 60 days ago. Leading indicators (leads by source, follow-up speed, meetings, pipeline created) tell the team whether the plan is working early enough to change it. One scorecard for sales and marketing together ends the argument about whose numbers are right.

## Order of operations

1. Do the goal math backwards from the revenue goal to weekly leads; mark assumed rates.
2. Write three objectives for the quarter and two or three key results each, every one measurable from a named source.
3. List the initiatives under each key result with owners and dates.
4. Build the scorecard: ten to fifteen rows, leading indicators at the top, a source and owner per row.
5. Run the weekly meeting on the scorecard; decisions written down.
6. Monthly review and report. 90-day review: score, learn, replace assumptions, reset the objectives.

## Who is involved

The founder sets the goal and sits in the weekly meeting. Whoever runs sales and whoever runs marketing both own rows. The go-to-market lead runs the meeting and writes the monthly note.

## How long it takes

A day to set up. Thirty minutes a week. An extra thirty a month. Half a day a quarter.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- The meeting has become a status update instead of a decision.
- A row has been unknown for a month.
- Revenue is debated and leading indicators are not read.
