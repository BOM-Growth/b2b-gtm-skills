# Team, hiring and handoff

Status: draft
Playbook 16 of 17. Stage: Operate. Usually runs after the 90-day review says the next step is a person, before the current owner steps back. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

The right next role, hired against a written scorecard, ramped on a 30-60-90 plan with dates, and handed a function documented well enough that it runs without the person who built it.

## Why it matters

The usual sequence is marketing person number one (a senior generalist or a fractional lead) sets the foundations, then an operator is hired to run them. Hiring the operator before the foundations exist produces activity without direction; hiring the senior person to run execution wastes them. Everything the role needs is written down before day one.

## Order of operations

1. Inventory the work: what gets done each week, by whom, written down or not, judgement or execution.
2. Decide the role from the inventory: operator, senior generalist, or a contractor for one specialty.
3. Write the role scorecard, then the job post from it.
4. Screen every candidate against the scorecard with a short task; tell everyone the outcome.
5. Build the 30-60-90 plan with a review at each boundary; one segment and one process first.
6. Prepare the handoff package with an index and a gap list; record walkthroughs of anything with steps.
7. Overlap for two weeks if possible, then review at 90 days.

## Who is involved

The founder decides the role and the pay. The current owner of the work writes the inventory and the handoff. The new hire's manager runs the ramp reviews.

## How long it takes

Two weeks to decide and post. Four to eight weeks to hire. Ninety days to ramp.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- The founder is still doing the execution six months after hiring for it.
- The new hire asks where things are in week three.
- A departure is coming and nothing is written down.
