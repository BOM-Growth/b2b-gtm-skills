# Discovery and diagnosis

Status: draft
Playbook 1 of 19. Stage: Foundation. Before everything else. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

A one-page read of where the company's go-to-market stands, what is leaking, and the three to five quick wins to do first. Plus the order in which to run the other playbooks.

## Why it matters

Whoever takes over marketing or go-to-market needs to learn everything before changing anything. Two to three weeks of reading, listening and pulling numbers gives a picture nobody in the company has had, and the quick wins start paying back while the plan is still being written.

## Order of operations

1. Read everything: product, site, deck, pricing, past plans and reports.
2. Listen: the last five to ten customer calls, the people who sell, the people who deliver, two customers if possible.
3. Pull the numbers for the last 90 days: traffic, leads by source, follow-up speed, meetings, deals, spend.
4. Score the eight areas 1 to 3 with evidence: positioning, website, sales process, CRM and tracking, follow-up, channels, content and proof, team and tools.
5. List the leaks with a size and a fix.
6. Pick the quick wins, assign owners and dates, start them.
7. Write the one page, review it with the founder, agree the playbook order.

## Who is involved

The person leading go-to-market does the reading and writes the page. The founder answers three questions on day one and signs off on the page. Whoever sells and whoever delivers each give an hour.

## How long it takes

Two to three weeks alongside normal work. Longer means researching instead of deciding.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- A new channel, product or segment has been added since the last read.
- The scorecard shows a leak the page did not name.
- Six months have passed.
