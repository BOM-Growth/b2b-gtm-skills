# Positioning and messaging

Status: draft
Playbook 4 of 19. Stage: Foundation. Usually runs after ICP and wedge, before offer and pricing, website, and sales enablement. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

A one-liner a stranger can repeat, and a positioning document the whole company writes from: what it sells, who it is for, what it replaces, why it is better, the problem and solution in plain words, three messaging pillars, what each buying role needs to hear, the objections with answers, and the proof.

## Why it matters

Positioning is the company strategy in one line: this customer, this problem, this solution, this difference. When it is written down, the website, the deck, the cold email, the demo and the job post all say the same thing. It is the best guess the company can make today; it goes to market and gets corrected by real conversations.

## Order of operations

1. Answer the four questions out loud with the founder, then write the one-liner and test it on three people outside the industry.
2. Interview the founder for fifteen minutes in their own words.
3. Write the four answers in full, the problem, the solution and the pillars.
4. Map the message to each buying role. Write the objection answers from real calls.
5. Collect proof and mark what is approved for public use. Draft testimonials for customers to approve with a yes or no.
6. Read it back with the founder, line edit, date and version it.

## Who is involved

The founder supplies the words and signs off. Whoever sells reviews the objections. The assistant drafts and keeps it consistent.

## How long it takes

The one-liner in an hour. The document in a session or two. Revisited every quarter and whenever a call says the market disagrees.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- Prospects describe the problem in words the document does not use.
- The same objection keeps winning.
- The website, the deck and the emails have drifted apart.
