# Offer and pricing

Status: draft
Playbook 5 of 19. Stage: Foundation. Usually runs after positioning and messaging, before website and sales enablement. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

A packaged offer the first customers can say yes to: what is included, what it costs and how that is worked out, the pilot or first step, the terms that matter, the one-sentence ask, and the proposal template every proposal starts from.

## Why it matters

Prospects stall at what does it cost when the answer changes every time. A written offer with a price anchored to what it replaces, and a first step small enough to say yes to, removes the stall and stops proposals from being rebuilt from scratch.

## Order of operations

1. Name the outcome in one sentence.
2. List what is included and what is not.
3. Pick the sizing measure the customer already knows.
4. Set the price between the replacement cost and the value delivered.
5. Design the pilot or first step with a number that proves it worked.
6. Write the terms page, the ask sentence, the offer page and the proposal template.
7. Sell it five times, then adjust.

## Who is involved

The founder sets the price. Whoever sells uses the ask and the template. Whoever delivers confirms what is included is deliverable.

## How long it takes

One working session for the first version. Reviewed after five sales and at each quarterly review.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- Every proposal still needs rewriting.
- Discounts are being given without a name.
- Prospects say yes to the demo and no to the price, or the reverse.
