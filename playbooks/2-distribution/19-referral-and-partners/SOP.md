# Referral and partners

Status: draft
Playbook 19. Stage: Distribution. Runs once there are happy customers and a few obvious partners. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

A referral program customers actually use, a tracker so every referral is credited and thanked, partner relationships with a lead exchange both ways, and one or two co-marketing campaigns a year that put the company in front of a partner's audience.

## Why it matters

Referred deals close faster and at a higher rate than any other source, and in a vertical market the partners (associations, complementary vendors, consultants, resellers) already have the trust the company is trying to earn. This is the cheapest channel and the most neglected because nobody owns it.

## Order of operations

1. Pick the referral mechanism and the ask; write the campaign and the thank-you.
2. Build the tracker: who referred whom, when, the status, the credit, the thank-you sent.
3. List candidate partners by how many of the company's buyers they already talk to; agree a lead exchange with the two or three best.
4. Plan one co-marketing campaign with each: a webinar, a guide, a joint case study.
5. Relaunch the referral ask each quarter; review partner results on the agreed cadence.

## Who is involved

The go-to-market lead owns the program and the partner relationships. Sales makes the referral ask at the right moment in every deal. Customer success asks at the 90-day review.

## How long it takes

A week to set up. A quarter to see the first referred deals. Ongoing after that.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- Referrals are happening and nobody is tracking or thanking them.
- A partner sends leads and gets nothing back.
- The ask has not been made in a quarter.
