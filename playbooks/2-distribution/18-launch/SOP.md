# Launch

Status: draft
Playbook 18 of 19. Stage: Distribution. Runs for each marketable release. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

Every release that changes a customer's day turned into a narrative, an internal brief before anything external, a calendar of what goes where, a kit for customers to roll it out, and a re-engagement run to the closed-lost deals the release answers.

## Why it matters

Product teams ship constantly and most of it never reaches a buyer. A launch rhythm turns the few releases that matter into pipeline: reactivated deals, demos booked on the news, and proof that the company moves. The internal brief comes first so sales never hears about a feature from a customer.

## Order of operations

1. Take the month's releases through the narrative step; pick the few that change a customer's day and what may be claimed about each.
2. Write the internal brief: what it is, who it is for, what to say, what not to promise, the demo path.
3. Set the launch calendar from the company's rhythm: tease, brief, announce, full launch, customer rollout.
4. Build the customer launch kit so existing customers can use the release without a call.
5. Run the closed-lost win-back to the deals that left for the reason this release fixes.
6. Read what the launch produced: demos, reactivated deals, replies, and file the result.

## Who is involved

Product supplies the release record. The go-to-market lead writes the narrative and runs the calendar. Sales gets the brief first. Customer success owns the rollout kit.

## How long it takes

A light launch in a week. A full launch in three to four weeks from brief to rollout.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- Sales hears about a release after customers do.
- Releases ship without anyone outside the product team noticing.
- Closed-lost deals that asked for the thing were never told it exists.

## Skills in this playbook

| --- | --- | --- |
| `release-to-narrative` | foundation | When a feature ships, is about to ship, or appears on the roadmap and marketing needs to know what it may say about it: "what can we say about X", "is this newsworthy", "write up the release", "what's the story on the new feature", "what's what changed for the customer". |
| `launch` | playbook | A marketable release: "we're launching", "plan the launch", "the new version", "when do we announce", "what goes out and when", "customers need to hear about this", "rebrand", "relaunch". |
| `launch-internal-brief` | play | Before anything about a release goes external: "brief the team", "what do we tell people if they ask", "talking points for the launch", "something goes out tomorrow", "the company needs to know this is happening". |
| `launch-calendar` | play | When a release will be marketed and nobody has landed the date: "when are we launching", "plan the launch dates", "tease, announce, launch", "what goes out when", "news got out early", "customers need to hear first". |
| `customer-launch-kit` | play | When a launch needs customers in it: "get customers posting on launch day", "customer quotes for the release", "co-marketing for the launch", "who can we ask to share", "the launch kit". |
