# Search, AEO and PR

Status: draft
Playbook 12 of 17. Stage: Distribution. Runs once the site carries the positioning. The steps below work with any SEO skill or tool the company already has.

## What it produces

The company found in Google and cited in AI answers for the terms the target customer actually searches, on pages that convert.

## Why it matters

Search is the capture channel: buyers who already know they have the problem look for a solution, and the company either shows up or a competitor does. AI answers now sit on top of search and pull from pages that state facts plainly. Both reward the same thing: specific pages, in the customer's words, with proof.

## Order of operations

1. List the terms from the customer language bank and from what sales hears, not from a keyword tool first. Then size them with a tool.
2. Audit the site for the basics: every page has one job, a title and description that say who it is for, loads fast, works on a phone, and has structured data where it helps.
3. Write or fix the pages that match the terms: the homepage, pillar pages, comparison pages against what the product replaces, and one plain-language article a month from the content engine.
4. State facts plainly on the page so AI answers can cite them: what it is, who it is for, what it costs, how it compares, with numbers and dates.
5. Measure in Search Console and the CRM: impressions, clicks, and leads with source organic. Review monthly.

## Who is involved

The go-to-market lead owns the term list and the page plan. Whoever writes content writes the pages. A developer or the site builder fixes technical issues.

## How long it takes

An audit in a week. Pages over a quarter. Results in three to six months; this is the slow channel.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- Positioning changed and the pages still say the old thing.
- Traffic is up and organic leads are not.
- A competitor's comparison page ranks for the company's own name.
