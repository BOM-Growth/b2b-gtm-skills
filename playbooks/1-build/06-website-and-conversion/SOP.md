# Website and conversion

Status: draft
Playbook 6 of 19. Stage: Build. Usually runs after positioning and the offer are written, before paid channels send traffic. Every skill in it works on its own.

## What it produces

A homepage that tells the wedge buyer in five seconds that this is for them, what it does and what to do next; the few pages around it; and every form and booking link landing in the CRM with a source.

## Why it matters

Messaging is the highest-impact thing on a website and most sites bury it. Design builds trust on a quick scan. A form that goes to an inbox is a leak. The site is written from the positioning document, so it changes when positioning changes.

## Order of operations

1. Set the job of each page in one sentence.
2. Write the homepage above the fold: headline, subheadline, one action, one proof line.
3. Write the rest of the homepage, the conversion page and the supporting pages from the positioning document.
4. Plan the forms and tracking so every lead reaches the CRM with a source.
5. Set the design direction, then build from the brief in whatever builder the company uses.
6. Test the headline with three people in the segment, test every form end to end, launch, note the date.
7. Run a conversion pass on live pages after 30 days of data.

## Who is involved

The go-to-market lead writes the copy and the brief. The founder approves the headline. A builder or the assistant builds. Whoever owns the CRM wires the forms.

## How long it takes

Copy in a week. Build in one to three weeks depending on the builder. Then monthly small improvements, not a redesign.

## Signs it needs revisiting

- Positioning changed and the site did not.
- Visitors come and do not convert, or leads arrive without a source.
- A new segment or offer needs its own page.
